1. Russian, Ukrainian and US officials began a second day of US-mediated peace talks in Abu Dhabi on Wednesday, the first direct talks between Moscow and Kyiv since February; both sides called the first day constructive, but Russia rejected a ceasefire, with no breakthrough reported. (Reuters)
2. Leaders from 102 countries gathered in Antalya as COP31 opened, with a pledge to raise electricity to 35% of global final energy by 2035. (UNFCCC)
3. British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army officer near Gloucester on Tuesday on suspicion of spying for Russia; he served with NATO's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps and remains in custody, uncharged. (CNN)
4. The US charged three men over an alleged Iran-backed plot to bomb RAF Fairford in September, part of a campaign of nearly 20 attacks; prosecutors say one of them directed it from a US jail. (AP)
5. The US House voted 211-204 to send eight articles of impeachment against Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth to the Judiciary Committee, blocking an immediate vote after Republican Thomas Massie forced it. (Reuters)
6. US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October and core prices 0.3%, above forecasts, lifting the odds of a Federal Reserve rate rise in December; no annual rate was published, as October 2025 data was never collected. (Reuters)
7. Anthropic set its IPO price range at $70 to $78 a share, to raise up to $64.35 billion at a valuation of up to $1.4 trillion, with trading expected on Nasdaq around 19 November. (CNBC)
8. Congo's Ebola outbreak passed 11,000 cases, with 5,387 deaths, the deadliest in the country and second-largest on record; no approved vaccine exists. (AP)
9. El Salvador's president said his troops destroyed the headquarters of Haiti's 400 Mawozo gang near Port-au-Prince; reports of 20 killed are unconfirmed, and the gang's leader was not there. (BBC)
10. A bus ploughed into 32 vehicles in Seongnam, South Korea, injuring 57 people, nine seriously; the driver told police the brakes had failed. (Yonhap)
